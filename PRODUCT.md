# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Students and staff at one school use Vulcan on a laptop or phone at the library door. Members sign themselves in and out and register their laptops. Gatekeepers run the door screens during busy periods and close incomplete visits. The librarian, an admin, manages gatekeepers and sees the full record.

## Product Purpose

Replace the paper library sign-in book with a durable digital ledger. The system records who entered, when they left, and which laptop came with them, so the library can account for its space and its equipment without adding friction at the door. Success looks like a short queue, an accurate open-visit list, and no unaccounted-for library laptops.

## Brand Personality

Quietly precise, collegiate, human. It should feel like a well-kept library ledger: warm paper, dark ink, ruled lines, and the occasional rubber stamp. Copy is plain and direct, never bureaucratic or cute.

## Anti-references

No generic SaaS dashboard aesthetics: no gradients, glass panels, oversized metric cards, purple accents, or identical card grids. No emoji, no Lucide icons, no spit-and-polish startup language. Avoid anything that feels like a visitor-management kiosk or a security checkpoint.

## Design Principles

1. The record comes first. Every screen makes the next ledger entry or the current state easy to read.
2. A door, not a dashboard. Primary actions are direct and quick enough to use while someone waits.
3. One system, two weights. Ordinary members see only what they need; gatekeepers and admins gain tools without changing the visual language.
4. Honest states. Open visits, borrowed laptops, and mismatches are named plainly, not hidden in badges or colour alone.
5. Paper memory. Materials and typography carry the ledger concept; familiarity and legibility win over decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All controls have 44px touch targets, visible focus states, usable keyboard paths, and text contrast of at least 4.5:1. Status is never conveyed by colour alone. Motion is limited to state feedback and is disabled or reduced under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The interface supports light and dark environments.
